CFS TECH MINUTE

WHAT IS CHANGING, AND WHY
When you sign in to your CFS account, Microsoft asks you to prove it is really you. Today many of us do that with a code sent by text message or with an automated phone call. Microsoft is retiring both for work accounts on February 1, 2027. The reason is security. Text codes and phone calls can be intercepted, and scammers have become very good at talking people out of them. Microsoft Authenticator is a free app that sends the sign in approval straight to your phone, protected by your phone’s own lock.

This is a Microsoft change, not a CFS one, and it applies to every organization that uses Microsoft 365. Our job is to make sure nobody at CFS is caught off guard by it.
WHAT HAPPENS ON FEBRUARY 1, 2027
If a text or a phone call is your only way to verify, the codes simply stop coming. The next time you sign in, Microsoft will stop you and require you to set up a passkey before you can reach your email, Teams, Paycom or anything else that uses your CFS login. Nobody loses their account, but the start of a shift is the worst possible moment to learn a new app.

Two related things to know. You may already have seen a screen asking you to set up a passkey right after you signed in. That is Microsoft starting this change early, and it is fine to follow it when it appears on a Microsoft sign in page you opened yourself. And if you reset your CFS password with a text message code, that changes on February 1 too. Authenticator works for password resets as well.
SET UP AUTHENTICATOR, STEP BY STEP
You will need your phone and a computer. Plan on about five minutes.
  1. Check where you stand. On a computer, go to aka.ms/mysecurityinfo and sign in with your CFS email. If Microsoft Authenticator is already in your list, skip to step 5. If you only see Phone, keep going.
  2. Install the app. On your phone, open the App Store or Google Play, search for Microsoft Authenticator, and install the free app from Microsoft Corporation. Open it and allow notifications when it asks.
  3. Start on the computer. Back on the Security info page, click Add sign in method, choose Microsoft Authenticator, and click Next until a QR code appears on the screen.
  4. Scan it with your phone. In the app, tap the + sign, choose Work or school account, then Scan QR code, and point your camera at the screen. Click Next on the computer and Microsoft sends a test request. Type the number shown on your computer into your phone and tap Yes.
  5. Make it your default. On the Security info page, next to Default sign in method, click Change and choose the Microsoft Authenticator notification. If Microsoft offers to set up a passkey, say yes and follow the prompts. It lives in the same app and unlocks with your face, fingerprint or phone PIN.
Using your personal phone? That is fine. The app does not give CFS access to your photos, texts, contacts or anything else on your phone. It only answers sign in requests for your CFS account.
NO SMARTPHONE, OR STUCK?
If you do not have a smartphone, or something does not look like what is described here, email the Help Desk at HelpDesk@cfsny.org. Tell us your name, your program and how you sign in today, and we will work out the right option with you. The same goes for a new or broken phone. Please do not wait until January. The sooner we hear from you, the easier it is for everyone.
Pro tip: A real Authenticator request only appears right after you sign in yourself. If your phone asks you to approve a sign in you did not start, tap No, it’s not me and let the Help Desk know. That one tap stops someone who has your password from getting any further.
