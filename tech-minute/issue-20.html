WHEN CONFIDENTIAL MAIL MAY LEAVE CFS
Every email and file at CFS can carry a sensitivity label. Internal - Confidential means what it says: for people inside CFS. If an email, or any file attached to it, carries that label and is addressed to someone outside cfsny.org, our data protection rule stops it before it leaves, and Outlook shows you a notice. Nothing is lost, and nobody is in trouble. The system did its job.

Sometimes, though, confidential material legitimately needs to go outside. That is fine when three things are true: the person is authorized to receive it, you send only what they need, and the message is protected on its way. Our Secure Email Policy (IT-SEC-002) spells this out. If you are not sure whether something should leave CFS at all, ask the Help Desk at HelpDesk@cfsny.org before you send. That question is always welcome.
FIVE STEPS THAT GET IT THERE SAFELY
When you do need to send confidential material to someone outside CFS, here is the routine:
  1. Start a new email. Not a reply and not a forward. A reply keeps the label of the message it answers, so a new message is the clean way to begin. Copy over any text you need.
  2. Clear the label on each attachment. Open each file in Word, Excel or PowerPoint, click Sensitivity on the ribbon, remove the Internal - Confidential label, type a short reason when it asks, then save and close. If a file is a PDF or you cannot change its label, stop there and contact the Help Desk. We will take care of that file for you.
  3. Label the email Confidential - External. On the new message, click Sensitivity and choose Confidential - External. If you do not see it in your list, use Options, Encrypt, Encrypt-Only instead. Both do the same thing: the message is encrypted so only the person you address can open it.
  4. Look above the To line. No yellow bar means you are clear. A yellow bar means one of the attachments still carries the Internal - Confidential label. Go back to step 2 for that file.
  5. Send, and give the recipient a heads up. They will receive a secure message and open it by signing in or with a one time passcode emailed to them. That is normal for encrypted mail, and a quick note from you keeps it from being mistaken for spam.
One more thing worth saying plainly: the label is there to protect the people we support, not to slow you down. If you ever get the notice and are not sure what to do, call the Help Desk. A two minute call beats a bounced email every time.
Pro tip: Attachments carry their own label, and Outlook copies the highest one onto your email automatically. That is why a message can say Confidential - External and still be stopped: a file inside it still says Internal - Confidential. Check the files first, then the email, and the yellow bar will not appear.
Try this with Charlie: Ask, “How do I send a confidential email to someone outside CFS?” and keep the answer handy for the next time a family member or outside agency needs something from you.
