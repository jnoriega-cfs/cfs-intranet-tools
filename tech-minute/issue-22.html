CFS TECH MINUTE
Issue #22 | November 17, 2026
WHY THIS YEAR IS DIFFERENT
The old advice was to look for spelling mistakes and awkward grammar. That no longer works. Scammers now use AI to write messages that are polished, friendly and personal, in any language, and to copy the look of real stores, banks and delivery companies. During the holidays they also have timing on their side. You really are expecting packages, receipts and requests, so a fake one does not look out of place.

The good news is that AI changes how a scam looks, not what it asks you to do. It still needs you to click, pay, sign in or keep a secret. That is where you catch it.
FOUR SCAMS TO WATCH FOR THIS SEASON
  1. The delivery problem text. A text says your package is on hold and asks for a small fee or a new address. Real carriers do not charge redelivery fees by text message.
  2. The order or payment problem. An email says your order was canceled or your card was declined, with a button to fix it. Open the store’s app or type its website yourself instead.
  3. The gift card request from “the boss.” A message that looks like it comes from a supervisor asks you to buy gift cards, send the numbers and keep it quiet. CFS leaders will never ask you to do this. Issue #19 showed how these work.
  4. The deal or charity that seems too good. Huge discounts, free gifts and urgent donation appeals, especially right after a big news story. Give through the charity’s own website, never through a link in a message.
THE CHECK STILL WORKS
Before you click, pay or reply, take the same three seconds from Issue #7:
  1. Who really sent it? Look at the actual email address or phone number, not just the name.
  2. Why the rush? “Within 24 hours,” “final notice” and “keep this between us” are pressure, not information.
  3. Where does it go? Hover over a link on a computer, or press and hold it on a phone, to see the real address. When in doubt, close the message and go to the website or app you already use.
If a suspicious message reaches your CFS email, do not click anything. Forward it to JNoriega@cfsny.org, the same address shown in the yellow banner at the top of outside emails, and then delete it. If it reaches your personal phone, delete it and do not reply, not even with STOP.
Pro tip: Waiting on a holiday order? Check it in the store’s or carrier’s own app, or by typing their website yourself. If the delivery problem is real, you will see it there too. If it is not there, the message was a scam.
