AN ATTACHMENT NEVER COMES BACK
When you attach a file, you create a second copy that now belongs to whoever receives it. It can be forwarded to anyone, saved anywhere, and it sits in their mailbox indefinitely. You cannot recall it, you cannot see who opened it, and you cannot undo it if it went to the wrong address.

That matters most when the document holds information about the people we support. Once that copy leaves, it is outside CFS permanently.

A link works differently. There is only ever one document, it stays in SharePoint, you can see exactly who has access, and you can take that access away whenever you need to.
THE RIGHT WAY TO SHARE
Four steps, and the third one is the one that matters:
  1. Save the document in SharePoint, in your department or program’s site. Not on your Desktop, and not in your personal OneDrive. Files in a SharePoint site belong to CFS and stay put when people change roles. Not sure which site is yours? Ask the Help Desk and we will point you to it.
  2. Open the document, click Share, then click the line at the top of the box that tells you who the link works for.
  3. Choose Specific people and type in exactly who needs it, and no one else. Typing Jim Noriega, for example, creates a link that works for me and for nobody I forward it to.
  4. Leave it set to Can view unless the person truly needs to make changes. Then send.
Do not skip past that box just because the link looks ready to go. Setting it yourself is what keeps the document limited to the people you named.

Want to practice first? Share a test document with me at JNoriega@cfsny.org and I will tell you exactly what I was able to open.
WHEN A LINK WILL NOT WORK
Sometimes it will not go smoothly. A family member may not have an account that works with the link. A partner agency’s system may block it. Someone may simply get stuck.

When that happens, please do not fall back on attaching the file. Contact the Help Desk and we will find a safe way to get the document where it needs to go.
Pro tip: Before you drop a document into an existing folder, check who that folder is already shared with. Open the folder, click the three dots, and choose Manage access. Anything you add inherits that access automatically, so a folder shared with an outside partner last spring will quietly share whatever you put in it today.
