WHY YOUR PHONE NEEDS EXTRA CARE THIS WEEK
Your phone carries your CFS email, Teams, Paycom and the Authenticator app that proves it is really you when you sign in. Away from home, it also connects to networks and chargers you do not control, and it is much easier to leave behind in a taxi, at a gate or under a restaurant table. A few simple habits keep a busy travel day from turning into a security problem.
FOUR HABITS FOR THE ROAD
  1. Skip open WiFi for anything important. Airport, hotel and coffee shop networks are open to anyone, and fake ones with names like “Airport_Free_WiFi” are easy to set up. For email, Paycom or banking, use your phone’s cellular data, or turn on your phone’s personal hotspot for your laptop.
  2. Deny any sign in request you did not start. If Microsoft Authenticator asks you to approve a sign in while you are at the dinner table, someone else may be trying your password. Tap No, it’s not me, then email HelpDesk@cfsny.org so we can check your account and help you change your password.
  3. Charge from the wall, not a public USB port. Bring your own charger and plug it into an outlet. A public USB port can carry data as well as power. If your phone asks to trust or allow access to a device while charging, choose Don’t Allow.
  4. Keep it locked, and report a lost phone fast. Use a PIN, fingerprint or face lock. If an agency phone, or any phone with your CFS email on it, is lost or stolen, email HelpDesk@cfsny.org right away, even on the holiday, and reset your CFS password yourself at aka.ms/sspr (Issue #9 showed how).
Pro tip: Setting an out of office reply before you leave? Keep it simple: say you are away, when you will be back and who to contact in the meantime. Leave out where you are going and how long you will be away. Scammers read automatic replies too.
Try this with Charlie: Ask, “What should I do if my agency phone is lost or stolen?” and save the answer in your notes before you travel.
