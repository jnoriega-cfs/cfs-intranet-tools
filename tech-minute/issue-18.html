STOP JUGGLING WINDOWS
If you've ever shared your screen to show a slideshow, you know the routine. Slides in one window, your notes printed out or hidden in another, and no way to see who's in the meeting or who just raised a hand.

PowerPoint Live fixes that. When you present a PowerPoint file directly in a Teams meeting, Teams gives you one window with everything in it: the current slide, your speaker notes (marked “Visible only to you”), a strip of every slide along the bottom, the people in the meeting, and a pen and highlighter for marking up the slide as you talk. Everyone else simply sees your slides.
TWO WAYS TO START
First, make sure your file is saved in OneDrive or SharePoint (one more reason not to save to your desktop). Join your Teams meeting, then pick whichever way is easier:
  1. From Teams. Click Share at the top of the meeting, and under PowerPoint Live, choose your file. Your recent OneDrive and SharePoint files are listed right there.
  2. From PowerPoint in your browser. With the deck open, go to the Slide Show tab and click Present in Teams, then click Present when Teams asks.
  3. Run the show. Use the arrows or the slide strip to move around, and the pen, highlighter, and laser pointer under the slide to point things out. Click Stop sharing when you're done.
Your audience gets a few extras, too. They can look back at a slide they missed without interrupting you, then click Sync to Presenter to catch up. They can even translate the slides into their own language or switch to high contrast, just for themselves. Want everyone on the same slide? Click Private view at the top of the meeting to keep people from browsing ahead. One heads up: if the meeting is recorded, the recording won't capture videos, animations, or anything you draw on the slides.
Pro tip: The first time you click Present in Teams from your browser, a box pops up asking to open Microsoft Teams. Click Open (and check the box to always allow it, so you aren't asked again). If PowerPoint then says Teams didn't open, wait a moment and click Try again in Teams. Or skip all of that and start from the Share button inside the meeting.
Try this with Charlie: Ask, “How do I present a PowerPoint in a Teams meeting so only I can see my notes?” Then start a Teams meeting by yourself and do a practice run before the real thing.
