One quick tip. About a minute to read. Something you can use today.

This Week’s Tip

The 3-second phishing check: three quick looks before you click. No security expertise required, just a habit that protects you and everyone we support.

Why it matters

Phishing emails used to give themselves away with typos and clumsy grammar. AI has fixed that for the scammers; researchers report that roughly four in ten phishing attempts this year are AI-generated, with perfect spelling and convincing branding. The good news: the tells that still work take only seconds to check, and they work no matter how polished the message looks.

The 3-second check

  1. Check the real sender address, not the display name. A message can say “CFS IT Support” while the actual address is something like support@cfs-alerts-secure.com. If it does not end in a domain you know and trust, stop.
  2. Pause on pressure. “Urgent,” “your account will be suspended,” “act within 2 hours”: urgency is the scammer’s favorite tool, because it pushes you to click before you think.
  3. Hover before you click. Rest your mouse over a link (on a phone, press and hold it) to preview where it really goes. If the address does not match where the email claims to send you, do not click.

Three quick looks: the sender’s real address, the pressure tactics, and where the link actually goes.

Pro tip: QR codes are the newest phishing trick (“quishing”). Never scan a QR code from an unexpected email or text; type the website address yourself instead. And when in doubt about any message, do not click. Forward it to HelpDesk@cfsny.org and we will check it for you. You will never be criticized for asking.
