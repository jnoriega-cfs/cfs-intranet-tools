Teams & Collaboration FAQ

Series 2 of 4 — Common Questions Answered

Chats
What's the difference between a Chat and a Channel?
▼

Chats are for quick, time-sensitive conversations:

  • 1-on-1 messages with colleagues
  • Small group threads (3-5 people)
  • Private discussions
  • Quick questions that need fast replies

Channels are for persistent team collaboration:

  • Department-wide communication
  • Project-based work
  • Shared files and documents
  • Long-term, searchable history
Rule of Thumb: If a new team member joined next month, would they need to see this conversation? Yes → Channel. No → Chat.
Chats
How do I start a 1-on-1 chat?
▼

Starting a chat is simple:

  1. Click the Chat icon in the left rail of Teams
  2. Click New Chat (top-left)
  3. Type the person's name in the search box
  4. Click their name to open a 1-on-1 chat
  5. Start typing your message
Tip: You can also click on someone's name anywhere in Teams (in a post, meeting, etc.) and select "Chat" to open a 1-on-1 with them.
Chats
Can I upgrade a 1-on-1 chat to a video or voice call?
▼

Yes! No scheduling required:

  1. Open the 1-on-1 chat
  2. Look at the top-right of the chat window
  3. Click the phone icon (for an audio call) or camera icon (for video)
  4. The other person will see an incoming call notification
Perfect for: Quick 5-minute questions that are easier to discuss verbally than to type out.
Chats
Can I share files in a chat? Where do they go?
▼

Yes, file sharing in chat is easy:

  1. Open the chat
  2. Click the attachment/clip icon at the bottom
  3. Select Upload from this device or Browse cloud storage
  4. Choose your file and send

Where it goes: Files automatically save to your OneDrive. The link stays with the conversation so it's searchable and easy to find later.

Tip: The file stays in both the chat history AND your OneDrive, so you never lose it even if you delete the chat.
Channels
How do I find or join a team and channel?
▼

There are several ways:

Option 1: Browse Teams

  • Click Teams in the left rail
  • Click Browse teams
  • Search for a team name
  • Click Join

Option 2: Get an Invite Link

  • Ask your team lead for the join link
  • Click the link and select Join

Option 3: Search for a Channel

  • Use the Search box at the top of Teams
  • Type the channel name
  • Click the result to preview and join
Tip: Once you join a team, you'll see all its channels in the left rail. Star your favorite channels to keep them at the top.
Channels
What's the difference between a Standard Channel and a Private Channel?
▼

Standard Channel (Public):

  • Anyone in the team can see and join
  • All team members automatically see it
  • Better for broad, team-wide communication
  • Example: "General," "Announcements," "OneDrive-SharePoint"

Private Channel:

  • Only invited members can access it
  • Restricted visibility — great for sensitive topics
  • Better for specific projects or sub-teams
  • Example: "Executive Leadership," "Confidential Audit," "Legal Review"
CFS Note: Use private channels for conversations involving sensitive client information, compliance matters, or internal personnel discussions.
Channels
How do I reply to a specific post in a channel?
▼

Use threaded replies to keep conversations organized:

  1. Hover over a post in the channel
  2. Click Reply in thread
  3. Type your response in the reply box
  4. Click Send

Why use threads?

  • Keeps related conversations grouped
  • Prevents the main channel from becoming noise
  • Makes it easy to follow a conversation from start to finish
  • Other team members can still see the thread and search for it later
Best Practice: Use threads for all channel replies. Only post directly in the channel for top-level announcements or new topics.
Channels
What are channel tabs and the Files tab?
▼

Every channel has multiple tabs at the top:

  • Posts — The main conversation thread
  • Files — All files shared in this channel (connected to SharePoint)
  • + tab icon — Add other apps like Planner, Wiki, or Power BI

The Files Tab:

This is essentially a SharePoint folder. Every channel automatically has one. When you upload a document, it's stored in both:

  • The channel conversation (for context)
  • The Files tab / SharePoint (for easy finding later)
Benefit: New team members joining the channel get instant access to all previous files and documents—no hunting through emails for old versions.
Meetings & Calls
How do I schedule a Teams meeting?
▼

You don't schedule Teams meetings separately—it's built into Outlook:

  1. Open Outlook Calendar
  2. Click New Event
  3. Add the event title and time
  4. Add attendees
  5. Look for "Add Teams meeting" button (usually in the ribbon or right panel)
  6. Click it — a Teams link is automatically inserted
  7. Send the meeting invite

What attendees see: They get an Outlook invite with a Teams link. They can click the link or join from Teams directly.

Tip: You don't need to copy-paste anything. The Teams link is automatically added when you check "Add Teams meeting."
Meetings & Calls
What's the difference between a scheduled meeting and a channel meeting?
▼

Scheduled Meeting (1-on-1 or small group):

  • Created in Outlook Calendar
  • Invite specific people via email
  • Recording goes to the organizer's OneDrive (private)
  • Only invitees can access the recording
  • Best for: 1-on-1s, closed meetings, sensitive discussions

Channel Meeting:

  • Scheduled directly in the channel
  • Everyone in the channel automatically sees the invite
  • Recording saves to the channel's SharePoint (team-accessible)
  • Anyone in the channel can find and watch the recording
  • Best for: Department updates, team standups, all-hands meetings
Decision Rule: Is this for the whole team? → Channel meeting. For specific people? → Scheduled meeting.
Meetings & Calls
How do I record a Teams meeting and where does the recording go?
▼

How to Record:

  1. Start or join a Teams meeting
  2. Look for the three-dot menu ⋯ (More options)
  3. Click Start recording
  4. You'll see "Recording" in red at the top of the meeting
  5. To stop: Click the three dots again and select Stop recording

Where the Recording Goes:

  • Channel meetings: Recording saves to the channel's SharePoint (visible to all channel members)
  • Scheduled meetings: Recording saves to the organizer's OneDrive (only for invitees)

What you get:

  • The video recording
  • An automatic transcript
  • An AI-generated summary
Best Practice: Always tell attendees you're recording at the start of the meeting (for transparency and respect).
Meetings & Calls
Can I join a Teams meeting from my phone?
▼

Yes, absolutely. Multiple options:

Option 1: Teams Mobile App

  • Download the Microsoft Teams app (iOS or Android)
  • Open the app and sign in with your CFS email
  • Tap Calendar
  • Tap the meeting from your calendar
  • Tap Join now

Option 2: Browser on Your Phone

  • Click the Teams meeting link from your email or calendar
  • Select Join on the web
  • Allow camera and microphone permissions
  • Click Join now

Option 3: Phone Call (Audio-Only)

  • Look for the Dial-in numbers option in the meeting invite
  • Call the provided number and enter the meeting ID
Bandwidth Tip: Phone calls use less data than video. If you're on a weak cell connection, audio-only may work better.
Meetings & Calls
Do guests need a Microsoft account to join a Teams meeting?
▼

No, they don't! This is one of Teams' biggest strengths.

When you invite someone outside CFS, they:

  • Receive a meeting invite link
  • Click the link
  • Enter their name
  • Join the meeting in their browser (no app required)

No sign-up, no account, no app download needed.

Perfect for: External consultants, partner agencies, family members in support groups, or anyone you need to meet with remotely.
Notifications
Why am I getting too many notifications?
▼

Teams has strong notification controls. Here's how to dial it back:

Step 1: Set Your Status

  • Click your profile picture (top-right)
  • Set your status to Busy, In a meeting, or Do Not Disturb
  • When you're busy, chat notifications pause until you're available again

Step 2: Enable Quiet Hours

  • Click your profile picture
  • Select Settings > Notifications
  • Scroll to Quiet Hours
  • Turn it ON and set your preferred times (e.g., 5 PM–8 AM)

Step 3: Mute Channels/Chats

  • Right-click a channel or chat
  • Select Mute
  • You'll still see the channel, but you won't get notifications

Step 4: @Mention Discipline

  • Only @mention people when it's truly urgent
  • Never use @channel or @team unless it's an all-hands announcement
Avoid Burnout: The world will not end if you don't respond in 5 minutes. Set your quiet hours and protect your evenings.
Notifications
How do I control notifications for a specific channel?
▼

Right-click the channel and choose your preference:

  • Mute — No notifications, but you can still see posts
  • Only show unread — You'll see it in the sidebar only if there are new messages
  • At mentions — Notify me only if someone @mentions me
  • All new posts — Notify me about everything (default)
Recommendation: For high-volume channels, use "At mentions" or "Mute" so you can focus on your work without constant interruptions.
Files & Sharing
Where should I store team files — chat, channel, or OneDrive?
▼

Files shared in CHAT:

  • Saved to your personal OneDrive
  • Best for: Quick document shares, one-off files
  • Downside: New team members won't see it

Files shared in CHANNELS:

  • Saved to the channel's SharePoint library (team-owned)
  • Best for: Project files, shared documents, team resources
  • Benefit: New team members get instant access to full history

Your Personal ONEDRIVE:

  • Your private storage area
  • Best for: Personal work, drafts, things you're still editing
  • Once ready to share: Move to a channel or share the link
Rule: Team project? → Channel. Personal draft? → OneDrive. Sharing with a small group? → Chat.
Files & Sharing
Can I co-edit documents with my team in Teams?
▼

Yes! Co-editing is seamless.

How it works:

  1. Upload a Word, Excel, or PowerPoint file to a channel
  2. Click the file in the channel
  3. Click Edit in browser (or Edit in Word/Excel for more features)
  4. Make your changes
  5. Changes save automatically — no emailing copies back and forth

What you see:

  • Real-time presence (see who else is editing)
  • Cursor indicators showing where others are typing
  • Version history (never lose an old version)
No More Version Hell: No more "Final_FINAL_v3_actualfinal.docx." Everyone edits one live file.
Best Practices
What are common Teams mistakes to avoid?
▼

DON'Ts:

  • Don't use group chat for team-wide topics. New team members can't see the history. Use a channel instead.
  • Don't @channel or @team for everything. Notification fatigue is real. Save broad mentions for actual all-hands news.
  • Don't schedule a meeting without an agenda. If there's no agenda, send a chat message instead and save everyone 30 minutes.
  • Don't keep notifications fully on 24/7. Burnout starts here. Set quiet hours. The world will not end.
  • Don't assume everyone is watching the channel. For urgent items, follow up with a direct message.
  • Don't share PHI or sensitive info in chat. Follow CFS HIPAA policy. Chat is not the right channel for protected information.
Best Practices
What should I DO in Teams?
▼

DOs:

  • Use channels for team work. If more than 3 people care about it, it belongs in a channel — not group chat.
  • @mention people deliberately. Tag specific people when you need their attention — don't @everyone.
  • Set your status & quiet hours. Mark yourself Busy or Do Not Disturb. Quiet hours protect your evenings.
  • Pin frequent chats and channels. Pinned items stay at the top — fast access to what you use most.
  • Record important meetings. So absent team members can catch up without a separate recap.
  • Use threads for channel replies. Reply in threads, not the main channel. Keeps conversations organized.
  • Search before you ask. Your question might already be answered in channel history.
Best Practices
How do I make a channel discoverable for new team members?
▼

Help your team find channels they need:

1. Write a Channel Description

  • Click the channel name at the top
  • Click Edit next to the channel description
  • Write what the channel is for: "General IT updates," "Copilot training support," etc.

2. Pin Important Posts

  • Hover over important posts
  • Click the three dots ⋯ and select Pin
  • Pinned posts appear at the top of the channel for easy reference

3. Use Consistent Naming

  • Use clear names: "OneDrive-Training" instead of "O365-Cloud-Stuff"
  • Avoid abbreviations unless everyone knows them
CFS & HIPAA
Where should I NOT put PHI or sensitive client information?
▼

NEVER put Protected Health Information (PHI) in:

  • Teams chat (even 1-on-1)
  • Channel posts
  • Unencrypted email

If you need to discuss sensitive information:

  • Call the person directly. Verbal communication is secure and immediate.
  • Use Therap/eVero for client documentation and care coordination.
  • Use SharePoint with encryption if you must share a document (contact IT).
CFS HIPAA Policy: Chat is not the right channel for protected information. Follow CFS HIPAA policy and contact HelpDesk@CFSNY.org if you're unsure whether something is okay to share.
CFS & HIPAA
Can I record a Teams meeting involving client information?
▼

Yes, but with considerations:

  • You CAN record internal CFS team meetings that discuss cases or clients in general terms
  • You MUST inform all attendees that you're recording (required by law in some states)
  • You MUST secure the recording so only authorized staff can access it
  • Recordings with PHI should be stored securely and deleted when no longer needed
When in doubt: Contact Jim Noriega (HIPAA Security Officer) or the HelpDesk before recording a meeting that involves client information.
CFS & HIPAA
I accidentally posted client info in a chat. What do I do?
▼

ACT IMMEDIATELY:

  1. Delete the message. Click the three dots ⋯ on your message and select "Delete."
  2. Notify the recipients. Send a follow-up chat: "I accidentally posted sensitive information. I have deleted it. Please disregard. Do not forward or share."
  3. Report to IT/Privacy Officer. Email HelpDesk@CFSNY.org or contact Jim Noriega with details of what happened.
  4. Document the incident. Note when it happened, what information was exposed, and how it was mitigated.

Even though you deleted it: Admins may still be able to recover deleted messages. That's why immediate notification and reporting is critical.

CFS Culture: Accidental breaches happen. Report it immediately so we can mitigate, document, and prevent it next time. We're here to support you, not punish you.
