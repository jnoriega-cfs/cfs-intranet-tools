Chats

What's the difference between a Chat and a Channel?

Chats are for quick, time-sensitive conversations with 1-on-1 or small groups (3-5 people). Chats are private and informal.

Channels are for persistent team collaboration. Everyone in the team can see it, and it has a searchable history that new team members can access.

Rule of Thumb: If a new team member joined next month, would they need to see this conversation? Yes → Channel. No → Chat.

How do I start a 1-on-1 chat?
  1. Click the Chat icon in the left rail of Teams
  2. Click New Chat
  3. Type the person's name
  4. Click their name to open a 1-on-1 chat
  5. Start typing your message
Can I upgrade a 1-on-1 chat to a video or voice call?

Yes! No scheduling required. Open the chat, look at the top-right corner, and click the phone icon (audio) or camera icon (video). The other person will see an incoming call notification.

Perfect for: Quick 5-minute questions that are easier to discuss verbally.
Can I share files in a chat? Where do they go?

Yes! Click the attachment icon at the bottom of the chat, select your file, and send. Files automatically save to your OneDrive and stay with the conversation so they're easy to find later.

Channels

How do I find or join a team and channel?

Option 1: Click Teams in the left rail → Browse teams → Search for the team → Click Join

Option 2: Ask your team lead for the join link and click it

Option 3: Use the search box at the top and search for the channel name

Once you join a team, star your favorite channels to keep them at the top.
What's the difference between a Standard Channel and a Private Channel?

Standard (Public) Channel: Anyone in the team can see and join. Best for broad team communication.

Private Channel: Only invited members can access it. Best for sensitive topics, projects, or sub-teams.

CFS Note: Use private channels for conversations involving sensitive client information or compliance matters.
How do I reply to a specific post in a channel?

Hover over a post and click Reply in thread. This keeps conversations organized and prevents the channel from becoming too noisy. Other team members can still see and search the thread.

What are channel tabs and the Files tab?

Every channel has multiple tabs at the top:

  • Posts — The main conversation
  • Files — All files shared in the channel (connected to SharePoint)
  • + icon — Add other apps like Planner, Wiki, or Power BI

New team members joining get instant access to all previous files — no hunting through emails!

Meetings & Calls

How do I schedule a Teams meeting?

Open Outlook Calendar, create a new event, add attendees, and look for "Add Teams meeting" button. A Teams link is automatically inserted. That's it!

You don't need to copy-paste anything — the Teams link is added automatically.
What's the difference between a scheduled meeting and a channel meeting?

Scheduled Meeting: Created in Outlook, invite specific people, recording goes to organizer's OneDrive (private). Best for 1-on-1s or closed meetings.

Channel Meeting: Scheduled in the channel, everyone in the channel sees it, recording goes to the channel's SharePoint (team-accessible). Best for department updates and team standups.

How do I record a Teams meeting and where does the recording go?

During the meeting, click the three-dot menu and select Start recording. You'll see "Recording" in red at the top. To stop, click the menu again and select Stop recording.

Where it goes:

  • Channel meetings → Channel's SharePoint (visible to all channel members)
  • Scheduled meetings → Organizer's OneDrive (only for invitees)

You also get an automatic transcript and AI-generated summary.

Always tell attendees you're recording at the start of the meeting (for transparency and respect).
Can I join a Teams meeting from my phone?

Yes! Download the Teams app (iOS or Android), sign in, go to Calendar, tap the meeting, and tap Join now. Or click the meeting link from your email and select Join on the web.

You can also dial in with the phone number from the meeting invite if you want audio-only.

Do guests need a Microsoft account to join a Teams meeting?

No! External guests can click the meeting link, enter their name, and join in their browser — no app or account required. Perfect for partners, consultants, or family members.

Notifications

Why am I getting too many notifications?

Step 1: Set your status. Click your profile picture and set status to Busy or Do Not Disturb. Notifications pause while you're busy.

Step 2: Enable Quiet Hours. Go to Settings > Notifications and turn on Quiet Hours (e.g., 5 PM–8 AM).

Step 3: Mute channels. Right-click a channel and select Mute — you'll still see it, but won't get notifications.

Step 4: Only @mention when truly urgent. Never use @channel or @team for routine updates.

The world will not end if you don't respond in 5 minutes. Set your quiet hours and protect your evenings!
How do I control notifications for a specific channel?

Right-click the channel and choose:

  • Mute — No notifications, but you can still see posts
  • Only show unread — You'll see it in the sidebar only if there are new messages
  • At mentions — Notify me only if someone @mentions me
  • All new posts — Notify me about everything (default)

Files & Sharing

Where should I store team files — chat, channel, or OneDrive?

Files in CHAT: Saved to your personal OneDrive. Best for quick shares. Downside: New team members won't see it.

Files in CHANNELS: Saved to the channel's SharePoint library. Best for project files and team resources. New team members get instant access to all previous files.

Your personal ONEDRIVE: Your private storage. Best for personal work and drafts. Move to a channel once ready to share.

Can I co-edit documents with my team in Teams?

Yes! Upload a Word, Excel, or PowerPoint file to a channel, click it, click Edit in browser (or Edit in Word/Excel), and edit together. Changes save automatically — no emailing copies back and forth.

You'll see who else is editing in real-time, with cursor indicators showing where they're typing.

Best Practices

What are common Teams mistakes to avoid?
  • Don't use group chat for team-wide topics. New team members can't see the history. Use a channel instead.
  • Don't @channel or @team for everything. Notification fatigue is real. Save broad mentions for all-hands announcements.
  • Don't schedule a meeting without an agenda. If there's no agenda, send a chat message instead. Save everyone 30 minutes.
  • Don't keep notifications fully on 24/7. Burnout starts here. Set quiet hours.
  • Don't share PHI or sensitive info in chat. Follow CFS HIPAA policy. Chat is not the right channel.
What should I DO in Teams?
  • Use channels for team work. If more than 3 people care about it, it belongs in a channel.
  • @mention people deliberately. Tag specific people when you need attention — don't @everyone.
  • Set your status & quiet hours. Mark yourself Busy or Do Not Disturb. Quiet hours protect your evenings.
  • Pin frequent chats and channels. Pinned items stay at the top for fast access.
  • Record important meetings. So absent team members can catch up without a separate recap.
  • Use threads for channel replies. Reply in threads, not the main channel. Keeps conversations organized.
  • Search before you ask. Your question might already be answered in channel history.
Where should I NOT put PHI or sensitive client information?

NEVER put Protected Health Information (PHI) in:

  • Teams chat (even 1-on-1)
  • Channel posts
  • Unencrypted email

If you need to discuss sensitive information:

  • Call the person directly — verbal communication is secure and immediate
  • Use Therap/eVero for client documentation and care coordination
  • Contact IT if you need encrypted file sharing options
Important: Chat is not the right channel for protected information. Follow CFS HIPAA policy and contact HelpDesk@CFSNY.org if unsure.
